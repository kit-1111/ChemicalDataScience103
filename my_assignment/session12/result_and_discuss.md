The hypothesis that higher lipophilicity leads to lower MIC was not supported. At XLogP = 3, the difference was not significant (p = 0.269), although thresholds of 1 and 2 showed significant differences in the opposite direction. Spearman correlation also indicated a weak positive association between XLogP and log MIC ( ρ = 0.245, p = 0.0028), suggesting that more lipophilic compounds tend to be less potent.

Higher hydrogen-bond acceptor counts and molecular weight were associated with lower MIC. Quinolone-like structures among the most potent compounds suggest that chemical scaffolds may influence these relationships, although this requires further investigation.

For antibiotic development against E. coli, lipophilicity should not be used as the sole selection criterion. These findings are limited by the small sample, mixed assay conditions, exploratory threshold selection, multiple comparisons and lack of toxicity data. Future work should use a larger dataset and account for chemical scaffold differences.
